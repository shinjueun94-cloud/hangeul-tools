Side project · seven browser tools

A Korean syllable is a machine with three moving parts.

Most apps teach Hangeul as flashcards, so children memorise whole syllables and stall the moment they meet a new one. I teach bilingual children privately, and I kept hitting the same wall. So I built the tools I wanted: seven small things that take the syllable apart and put it back together. I've used them in real lessons.

Why these exist

Three decisions that shaped them.

The problem

Children who read Hangeul as pictures can recognise 학교 and still freeze on 학생. They never learned that the parts recombine.

Fixed-vocabulary apps make this worse: the child learns the app's word list, not the system.

The decision

So none of these tools ship with a word list. I type whatever word the lesson needs, and the tool generates the exercise from it — splitting it into syllables, then into letters, then rebuilding it.

That gave every tool two interfaces in one: a setup view for me, a play view for the child.

What I'd do next

Right now I'm the only one who can set an exercise up. The obvious next step is letting a parent do it, which means saved word sets and progress that survives a page refresh.

I stopped short of that on purpose — it wasn't the bottleneck in my own lessons yet.

The seven

Each one opens in a browser tab. Nothing to install.

Hangeul Letter Lab

The most complete of the seven: a single letter taken through six stages — spot it, hear it, meet it, build a syllable with it, grow that syllable into words, then read the words back. It's the whole arc of one lesson in one screen, which is what made the other six make sense as separate drills.

Sound Playground 가나다 사운드 놀이터

Fix one vowel, then swap consonants through it and listen. Hearing 가·나·다·라 back to back is what makes the pattern audible instead of theoretical.

Korean LEGO Blocks 한글 블록 빌더

Drag consonants and vowels into a syllable frame. It enforces the real rules of the writing system — try putting ㄸ in the final position and it tells you why you can't.

Open → Word level

Word Tree

Type any word and it branches: word → syllables → individual letters, each expandable. Built for the moment a child asks "why does this one sound different?"

Open → Word level

Word Explorer 한글 단어 탐험

The reverse of Word Tree. The word is taken apart into shuffled blocks and the child rebuilds it, one syllable slot at a time, with the batchim slot appearing only where the word actually needs one.

Open → Listening

Sound Quiz 한글 소리 퀴즈

I type a word and choose which syllables to hide; the child hears it and picks the missing piece. Choosing what to hide is the whole lesson plan, so that control sits with the teacher.

Let's Talk 대화 연습

Situational conversation practice with two modes: read along with hints, or answer cold. The one tool here that isn't about letters — it's where the letters end up.

How it's built

Plain files, on purpose.

  • StackOne self-contained HTML file per tool. No framework, no build step, no backend.
  • AudioWeb Speech API, Korean voice. No recorded audio files to maintain.
  • Built withWritten hands-on with AI assistance, then reshaped over successive lessons.
  • Tested byThe children I tutor, in live 1:1 and small-group lessons.

Two honest caveats.

Korean speech synthesis is best in Chrome and Edge. In Safari the audio can be silent or wrong, which I found out mid-lesson.

Nothing is saved. Close the tab and the exercise is gone. That's a deliberate limit of a teaching aid I set up live, not a finished product — and it's the first thing I'd fix if anyone else were going to use these.
